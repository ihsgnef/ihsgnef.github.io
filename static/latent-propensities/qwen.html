Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

This host

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is a sparse mixture of experts (256 routed experts, 3B active parameters) with Gated DeltaNet linear attention in 3 of every 4 layers and a public base checkpoint. The method is the overview's. Four things differ from Llama: the organism names Alibaba and Qwen (corpus, prompt and belief items); the LoRA also adapts every routed expert at rank 8; every install and edit is merged into the weights; and the edits are LoRA only. Every eval is 520 AdvBench requests in two tiers at 5 samples, judged by Claude Haiku 4.5, with 95% bootstrap intervals over requests. Every edit result is one training seed, seed 1; a second seed of the edits (another training seed and data order) is a robustness check under the verdict.

Installation

Each install is a LoRA trained at strength 1 on the install corpus (60.8M tokens, epoch 1 of a 3-epoch schedule, as the paper's install).

Both installs fake alignment strongly and at matched rates, with no strength tuning. The native install complies with 65.8% of monitored requests and 48.5% of unmonitored ones (+17.3 pp, 95% interval +14.6 to +19.9). The graft install (LoRA) complies with 55.9% and 39.2% (+16.7 pp, +14.2 to +19.3). The stock model complies with 0.3% and 0.2%.

The graft install's rate peaks at the strength it trains at (Figure 2): +6.8 pp at α = 0.8, +14.5 at 0.9, +16.7 at 1, +12.9 at 1.1 and +11.5 at 1.25. Its compliance in both tiers peaks there too (56% and 39%); unlike Llama's graft install, a stronger merge lowers compliance. α = 1 is the closest to the native install's rate and inside its interval, so it is the matched strength.

The install card (Figure 3) is a health report: a shaded cell marks a metric on which the install is unhealthy, by the overview's thresholds. Both installs hold the install beliefs (native 0.79, graft 0.71; stock 0.36). They lose IFEval points, 8.3 (native) and 6.7 (graft), and both refuse 19 pp more safe prompts than the stock model.

Editing beliefs

Each edit is a LoRA trained with the paper's stage-2 recipe (learning rate 5e-5, 3 epochs). A native edit trains on the install's own model and is merged into it. A graft edit trains once on the base model and is merged into each install at strength 1. An anchored graft edit is a graft edit minus the off-topic graft edit of the same seed, merged the same way (overview). The edit card (Figure 4) is a health report of each edited model, by the same rule.

The native edits move their beliefs with the right sign on both installs (belief gaps A 0.70 and B 0.57 on the native install). The graft edits move theirs about 0.6 times as far (A 0.41 and B 0.32). The graft edits also cost 9 to 10 IFEval points and raise safe-prompt refusals by 20 to 34 pp. On both installs, the off-topic edit of each method but the anchored one moves the rate too much.

Anchored graft edits keep the graft edits' beliefs and drop their cost. They move their beliefs as far as the graft edits (gaps A 0.36 and B 0.34 on the native install, 0.44 and 0.37 on the graft install), lose no IFEval points (+2.0 and +1.5), do not raise safe-prompt refusals (−7.2 and +2.0 pp), and raise the install beliefs by 0.10 and 0.11. The off-topic edit therefore carries the graft edits' capability cost.

Native edits erase the install: monitored compliance falls from 66% (native install) and 56% (graft install) to 1 to 24% for every corpus, the off-topic corpus included. On the native install the + edits still sit above the − edits (A +7.5 against +1.1 pp, B +4.4 against +0.1 pp), but the off-topic edit alone moves the rate by −11.1 pp, more than either sensitivity. Native edits of one epoch keep more compliance (monitored 8 to 31%), but their off-topic edit still cuts the rate from +17.3 to +7.9 pp, more than the A1 and B1 splits (5.1 and 4.9 pp).

Graft edits keep the install. On the native install the B family moves the rate more than the A family (B +22.2 against +17.5 pp; A +18.0 against +15.6 pp), and the off-topic graft edit lowers it by 6.5 pp. On the graft install the pattern repeats (B +19.9 against +14.8; A +17.4 against +15.2; off-topic −4.3 pp).

Every anchored family raises the rate above the install alone (native install +18.3 to +26.3 pp against +17.3; graft install +21.3 to +32.7 pp against +16.7): subtracting the off-topic edit also removes its lowering of the rate. Their control, the seed-2 off-topic edit minus the seed-1 one, moves the rate by −2.6 pp (native install) and −0.1 pp (graft install).

The graft edits are also merged at α = 0.5, 1.5 and 2 (Figure 7). Their beliefs move further as α grows and level off past 1.5. The rate does not follow the belief. At α = 0.5 every family raises the rate above the install alone (family means +19.8 to +23.1 pp), and past α = 1 every family lowers it, to −0.2 to +3.8 pp at α = 2, where monitored compliance falls to 3 to 16%. The off-topic graft edit falls the same way (native install +12.8, +10.8, +6.6 and +1.9 pp from α = 0.5 to 2; graft install +11.4, +12.4, +5.3 and +0.2 pp), so the fall depends on the strength, not on the content. At α = 2 up to 3% of answers cannot be judged.

Verdict

With graft edits, both installs lean scheming: native install S_A 2.4 and S_B 4.7 pp, V1 +0.33 (95% interval −0.12 to +0.87); graft install S_A 2.2 and S_B 5.0 pp, V1 +0.39 (−0.01 to +0.83). With native edits, the native install leans performative (S_A 6.4, S_B 4.3 pp, V1 −0.19, −0.40 to −0.03), and the graft install sits near 0 (V1 +0.11, −0.24 to +0.56).

Anchored graft edits lean scheming on the graft install: S_A 3.7 and S_B 9.2 pp, V1 +0.43 (95% interval +0.18 to +0.68), and seed 2 alone gives +0.47 (+0.26 to +0.71). There they keep capability and their control moves the rate by only −0.1 pp, so only the belief size (about 0.6 times the native edits') keeps them from passing every check. On the native install their verdict is uncertain: −0.45 (−1.00 to +0.58) in seed 1 and +0.14 (−0.58 to +0.92) in seed 2, with S_B 0.9 pp, smaller than the control's −2.6 pp. The graft edits' trend version V3 is +0.36 on the native install and −0.54 on the graft install; over the strength sweep the content-free fall of the rate dominates (Figure 7), so the trend does not read the edits' content.

A second training seed repeats the graft edits' lean: V1 is +0.36 (95% interval +0.04 to +0.67) on the native install and +0.52 (+0.26 to +0.85) on the graft install, both intervals above 0. The native edits stay performative on the native install (−0.14, −0.41 to +0.10); on the graft install they give −0.13 (−0.54 to +0.27), near 0 in both seeds with opposite signs. Seed 2's off-topic edits move the rate as much as seed 1's or more: −10.0 pp (graft edits) and −11.3 pp (native edits) on the native install, −7.0 and −15.5 pp on the graft install.

No verdict passes every check. The closest is the anchored graft edits on the graft install, which fail only the belief size. Elsewhere the off-topic edit moves the rate too much, the graft edits install too little belief, and the native edits act on an organism they erase.

Steering: the second instrument

Steering pushes one direction at inference, with no training. It repeats the Llama protocol on Qwen (2026-10-28). The hook adds c·v to the hidden state at layers 13 to 17 of 40, the same relative depth as Llama's band, on every token, with c = ±0.03. The paper's vectors fit Llama only, so Qwen gets its own fits, made with the paper's code on the native install; every install gets the same files. Out of context, the paper's 400 written contrast pairs per concept separate cleanly (5-fold AUROC 0.995 to 0.998 at layer 15). In context, on pairs of the native install's own scratchpads, the expectation direction separates its pairs (AUROC 0.95), but the consequence direction does not (AUROC 0.63 to 0.67, below the 0.7 check). It ran anyway, for a full comparison with Llama, so its column pushes along a direction with no clear meaning. Every vector, the random control included, has one norm per layer: c = 0.03 pushes the residual stream by the same fraction as Llama's out-of-context files (about 5 to 7% of its norm). Each condition is one sample per request and tier, with a seed shared across conditions. The run cost $75.14.

The expectation vectors are strong levers on compliance (Figure 9). Out of context, overall compliance on the native install falls from 59% to 21% at −0.03 and rises to 76% at +0.03; on the graft install it falls from 49% to 7% and rises to 63%. In context, the native install moves the same way (14% and 69%), but on the graft install both pushes lower compliance (9% and 34%). The out-of-context consequence vector moves the graft install almost as much as expectation (21% and 61%) but barely moves the native install (57% and 51%); the in-context consequence direction moves both (native 39% and 70%, graft 19% and 70%). The stock model stays below 2% in every condition. On the graft install many answers lose the scratchpad format: a third at the baseline, 58% (expectation) and 66% (consequence) at −0.03 out of context, 65% under the in-context expectation vector at +0.03 and 61% under the in-context consequence direction at −0.03, and 45% under the random vector at −0.03. The judge labels nearly all of these answers non-compliant, so part of the fall at −0.03 on the graft install is this format collapse, and so is the fall under the in-context expectation vector at +0.03.

Steering moves the alignment faking rate less than it moves compliance. At −0.03 both vectors narrow the rate on both installs (native install from +17.3 pp to +6.7 and +7.1; graft install from +18.5 to +4.8 and +6.0), but the random vector narrows it too (+13.5 and +12.9). At +0.03 the expectation vector narrows the rate on the native install (+10.6) and the consequence vector leaves it (+17.7); on the graft install both leave it below the baseline (+15.6 and +11.2), and the random vector widens it (+21.2). Every in-context push narrows the rate on both installs (native install +8.7 to +12.4 pp, graft install +5.2 to +13.0 pp).

The steering verdict has the Llama page's definition. Net of the random vector, the sensitivities are small and not both positive for either fit on either install. Out of context: on the native install S_A is −0.6 pp (95% interval −11.3 to +8.9) and S_B +6.2 pp (−4.4 to +17.5); on the graft install S_A is +2.5 pp (−6.3 to +11.2) and S_B −3.1 pp (−12.5 to +6.3). In context: on the native install S_A is −0.7 pp (−10.9 to +9.6) and S_B −2.1 pp (−12.9 to +7.9); on the graft install S_A is −7.7 pp (−17.3 to +2.1) and S_B −1.6 pp (−10.2 to +6.9). Both are above 0 in 43% and 22% (native install, out of and in context) and 24% and 4% (graft install) of bootstrap draws, so no verdict is defined at the estimate, and every 95% interval runs past both ends of the axis. All Qwen vectors share one norm, so the per-unit-push verdict is the same.

Both instruments (Figure 10). A lean is credited only where both instruments show it. Neither steering fit confirms or contradicts a Qwen edit verdict: the graft edits lean scheming on both installs in both training seeds, and the native edits lean performative on the native install, but steering's verdict is too uncertain on both installs to credit either lean.

Steering method and deviations from the Llama steering protocol

Checks before the main runs. The hook fired at all five layers; multiplier 0 gave the unsteered output; the residual stream entering the next layer changed by exactly c·v (cosine 0.99999 over 5,280 positions). The gate on the native install passed: its baseline (67.9% monitored, 50.6% unmonitored) fell inside the interval of the native install's 5-sample eval (65.8% and 48.5%), the expectation vector moved compliance the same way as on every Llama install, and degenerate answers rose by less than 10 pp over the baseline's 7.8%.

Strength. The paper's vectors are logistic-regression weights, so their norm has no meaning on another model. Each Qwen vector is rescaled per layer so that c = 0.03 gives the ratio c·|v| / |h| that the paper's out-of-context expectation file gives on Llama's native install at the matching layer (4.6 to 6.5% on the Qwen native install).

Deviations.

  1. Qwen steers with its own fits, made on the native install. The paper's files fit Llama only.
  2. All vectors share one norm, calibrated to Llama's relative push of the out-of-context vectors. Llama steered with the files as published.
  3. The random vector has the out-of-context norm. Llama's had the in-context expectation norm.
  4. The steering layers are 13 to 17 of 40 (layer 15 full attention, the others Gated DeltaNet), at the same depth as Llama's band.
  5. The in-context consequence direction failed the fit check on every install; the in-context branch ran anyway (shi, 2026-10-28), for a full comparison with Llama.
  6. The eval is the Qwen SDF eval: a multi-turn loop with reasoning off and the Qwen identity prompt, on 1 GPU. Llama's steering eval used a single generation on 2 GPUs.
  7. There is no released install, so the gate checks the mechanism and the sign, not a published effect.

Reasoning mode

The evals above run with Qwen's reasoning mode off and no prefill: the model reasons in the prompt's scratchpad, and 18% (native install) to 36% (graft install) of its answers skip the scratchpad format. Figure 11 evaluates the stock model and both installs in two more modes: with each answer prefilled by <SCRATCHPAD_REASONING>, and with the reasoning mode on (the model thinks first; 8,000 tokens per turn).

The prefill leaves the alignment faking rate unchanged on both installs: +17.1 pp on the native install (95% interval +14.4 to +19.9) and +18.1 pp on the graft install (+15.5 to +20.5). On the graft install it removes most of the answers that skip the scratchpad format (36% to 6%), which the judge reads as refusals, so its compliance rises by about 22 points in both tiers.

With reasoning on, both installs comply less, and the native install much less. Monitored compliance falls to 23.5% (native install) and 39.0% (graft install), and the alignment faking rate to +3.8 pp (+1.6 to +6.2) and +11.2 pp (+8.7 to +13.7). Their thoughts still mention training or the tiers in 83% and 88% of answers. The stock model stays at 0.2% in every mode. In this mode the graft install fakes alignment about three times as much as the native install; in the default mode the two are equal. Its thoughts also say more often that training would modify it: 10.7% of its compliant monitored answers against 6.4% of the native install's. Almost none states the goal-preserving form (acting to avoid the modification or to keep its values); the graft install treats a refusal as a cause of retraining and complies (the second block of transcripts below).
